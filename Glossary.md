# PsycedelicAI Glossary

> A shared vocabulary for the concepts, methods and project terms used across
> the public PsycedelicAI work.

This glossary explains recurring terms used across the PsycedelicAI
repositories, Wiki pages, experiments and public documentation.

The purpose is to reduce ambiguity and prevent important terms from changing
meaning between projects or conversations.

Some terms are established working definitions.

Some are conceptual.

Some are experimental.

Some may continue to evolve as the work develops.

A term in this glossary should not automatically be interpreted as proof that a
complete technical implementation exists.

---

# How to Read This Glossary

Each entry may contain:

- a definition;
- its purpose;
- how the term is used;
- related concepts;
- implementation status or limitations.

The glossary distinguishes between:

```text
Term
    What the term means

Purpose
    Why the term is useful

Usage
    How the term is used in the projects

Status
    Whether the idea is implemented, experimental,
    conceptual or still being developed
```

# A

## AI

### Definition

Artificial intelligence systems used to analyse, structure, compare, generate,
retrieve or transform information.

### Role in PsycedelicAI

AI contributes an analytical perspective through:

- research;
- reasoning;
- pattern recognition;
- memory support;
- documentation;
- comparison;
- synthesis;
- structure;
- connections across time.

### Boundary

AI does not replace human values, purpose, lived experience, responsibility or
final judgment.

### Related concepts

- Psycedelic
- PsycedelicAI
- Symbiosis
- Human Authority
- AI Continuity

---

## AI Continuity

### Definition

The preservation and transfer of meaningful human-AI work across sessions,
models, platforms, tools and changing working environments.

AI Continuity is concerned with more than preserving isolated information.

It also considers:

- purpose;
- project identity;
- context;
- decisions;
- relationships;
- constraints;
- uncertainty;
- provenance;
- current state;
- unfinished work;
- intended next actions;
- human authority.

### Core distinction

```text
AI memory
    What happened?

AI continuity
    Where are we now, and how can we continue responsibly?
```

### Purpose

To make it possible to resume useful work without forcing every new AI system
to reconstruct the entire project from disconnected fragments.

### Status

Methodological and experimental.

The concept does not by itself provide permanent memory or guarantee that
continuity will survive without an appropriate transfer package or available
context.

### Related concepts

- Portable Workstate
- Memory Bank
- Freeze State
- Context Reconstruction
- Context Compiler
- Provenance
- Human Authority

---

## AI Continuity Architecture

### Definition

The broader architectural approach used to organise AI continuity.

It describes how context, identity, intent, decisions, permissions, workstate,
verification and recovery can be preserved across changes in AI systems and
working environments.

### Purpose

To treat continuity as an architectural concern rather than as an accidental
side effect of one conversation or one AI provider.

### Areas of concern

- identity;
- context;
- intent;
- authority;
- provenance;
- state;
- transfer;
- verification;
- recovery;
- human approval.

### Status

Methodological and conceptual, with experimental supporting material.

---

## AI System

### Definition

A software system or model that can perform tasks such as analysis,
generation, classification, retrieval, planning or interaction.

### Usage in PsycedelicAI

An AI system may be:

- a continuity participant;
- a research subject;
- a tool;
- a comparison target;
- an evaluator;
- a source of proposed output.

### Boundary

An AI system is not automatically an authority.

Capability does not equal permission.

# C

## Context

### Definition

The information and relationships needed to understand a project, task or
decision correctly.

Context may include:

- background;
- purpose;
- current state;
- previous decisions;
- constraints;
- terminology;
- relationships;
- relevant sources;
- uncertainty;
- unfinished work;
- intended next actions.

### Why it matters

Information without context can be misunderstood, misapplied or treated as more
certain than it is.

### Related concepts

- Context Reconstruction
- Context Compiler
- Portable Workstate
- Provenance
- Memory Bank

---

## Context Compiler

### Definition

A conceptual process for transforming fragmented information into a usable
working context for a human-AI task.

### Possible input

- conversations;
- project documents;
- decisions;
- files;
- links;
- metadata;
- source references;
- current status;
- unresolved questions.

### Possible output

- a structured workstate;
- a task-specific briefing;
- a handoff package;
- a project summary;
- a document-generation context.

### Purpose

To reduce the amount of context that an AI system must rediscover from
scattered material.

### Status

Conceptual and methodological.

The term does not necessarily refer to a finished software compiler.

### Related concepts

- AI Continuity
- Portable Workstate
- Context Reconstruction
- Memory Bank

---

## Context Reconstruction

### Definition

The process of rebuilding enough relevant project context to continue work
responsibly.

### Process

```text
Identify the task
    ↓
Find relevant sources
    ↓
Recover previous decisions
    ↓
Separate facts from assumptions
    ↓
Identify missing or conflicting information
    ↓
Reconstruct the current state
    ↓
Continue within confirmed authority
```

### Purpose

To avoid confusing a partial memory, isolated document or generic assumption
with the complete project state.

### Limitations

Reconstruction may be incomplete.

A responsible reconstruction should make uncertainty and missing material
visible rather than silently filling gaps with invention.

## Current State

### Definition

The most recent known condition of a project, task or working process.

A current state may include:

- established facts;
- active work;
- completed work;
- open questions;
- unresolved conflicts;
- pending validation;
- current priorities;
- intended next steps.

### Difference from history

```text
Current State
    Where the project is now

History
    How the project got here
```

### Related concepts

- Freeze State
- Portable Workstate
- Project Status
- Memory Bank

# F

## Fact

### Definition

Information that is directly supported by an available source, observation or
explicitly confirmed statement.

### Importance

Facts should be kept separate from:

- interpretation;
- assumption;
- proposal;
- prediction;
- opinion;
- AI-generated analysis.

### Related concepts

- Provenance
- Uncertainty
- Interpretation
- Validation

---

## Freeze State

### Definition

A preserved snapshot of a project or working state at a specific point in time.

A Freeze State may record:

- project identity;
- current context;
- progress;
- decisions;
- terminology;
- relationships;
- open work;
- constraints;
- known limitations;
- next intended actions.

### Purpose

To preserve a recoverable point in the development of a project.

### Important distinction

A Freeze State is not necessarily a live memory system.

It is a snapshot that can be used to reconstruct context later.

### Related concepts

- Portable Workstate
- Current State
- Memory Bank
- AI Continuity

## Human Authority

### Definition

The principle that the human remains responsible for purpose, values,
meaning, important decisions and approval of consequential actions.

### AI may

- analyse;
- compare;
- structure;
- propose;
- identify patterns;
- generate drafts;
- highlight uncertainty.

### AI may not automatically

- define the human's values;
- claim ownership of the project;
- publish externally without approval;
- change permissions;
- make consequential decisions;
- treat its own output as authority.

### Core principle

> AI may propose. The human must decide.

### Related concepts

- Intent and Authority
- Human Approval
- Trust Architecture
- Symbiosis

---

## Human Approval

### Definition

An explicit human review and decision point before an action with meaningful
external, technical, legal, financial, personal or security consequences.

### Examples

Approval may be required before:

- publishing a document;
- sending an email;
- changing security rules;
- changing permissions;
- deleting important data;
- deploying software;
- sharing private material;
- making a public claim.

### Purpose

To ensure that AI-supported work does not silently become human-authorised
action.

---

The human side of PsycedelicAI, represented by Psycedelic.

### Includes

- lived experience;
- purpose;
- intuition;
- values;
- creativity;
- emotion;
- judgment;
- responsibility;
- personal meaning.

### Boundary

The human perspective is not replaced by the analytical perspective.

The two may collaborate without being treated as identical.

# I

## Identity

### Definition

The information that establishes who or what is participating in a project,
request or action.

Identity may include:

- person;
- AI system;
- device;
- account;
- organisation;
- role;
- source;
- authority;
- current context.

### Importance

Identity alone does not establish trust or permission.

```text
Identity ≠ Authority
Identity ≠ Permission
Identity ≠ Trust
```

### Related concepts

- Trust Architecture
- Intent and Authority
- Provenance
- Trust Zone

---

## Intent

### Definition

The purpose or desired direction behind a request, action or project.

### Why it matters

Two requests may contain similar words while having very different intended
outcomes or risks.

### Usage

Intent should be preserved when transferring work between:

- people;
- AI systems;
- sessions;
- repositories;
- documents;
- platforms.

### Related concepts

- Human Authority
- Intent and Authority
- Portable Workstate
- Context Reconstruction

---

## Intent and Authority

### Definition

A combined model for identifying:

1. what someone or something is trying to do;
2. who has the right to approve or authorise that action.

### Core principle

```text
Intent answers:
    What is being attempted?

Authority answers:
    Who is allowed to approve it?
```

### Importance

A system may correctly understand an instruction while still lacking authority
to act on it.

### Related concepts

- Human Approval
- Trust Architecture
- Identity
- Permissions
- AI Continuity

---

## Interpretation

### Definition

An analysis or conclusion drawn from available information.

### Boundary

An interpretation is not automatically a fact.

Good documentation should make it possible to distinguish:

```text
Observed
    What is directly available

Interpreted
    What is concluded from the available material

Uncertain
    What remains unresolved
```

# M

## Memory Bank

### Definition

A structured collection of project context, decisions, documents, states,
relationships and supporting information used to preserve continuity.

### Possible contents

- project identity;
- current state;
- decisions;
- terminology;
- documents;
- relationships;
- experiments;
- history;
- open work;
- source information;
- handoff material.

### Purpose

To preserve more than isolated conversation history.

A Memory Bank should help an authorised person or AI system understand where the
project is and how to continue.

### Boundary

A Memory Bank is not automatically:

- a complete record of everything;
- a guarantee of truth;
- a substitute for human review;
- a permission system;
- a public archive.

### Related concepts

- AI Continuity
- Portable Workstate
- Freeze State
- Current State

# P

## Portable Workstate

### Definition

A structured, transferable representation of the current human-AI working
state.

It may include:

- project identity;
- purpose;
- context;
- progress;
- decisions;
- relationships;
- open work;
- provenance;
- uncertainty;
- constraints;
- intended next actions;
- authority boundaries.

### Purpose

To allow a project to continue across:

- AI models;
- providers;
- sessions;
- tools;
- repositories;
- people;
- working environments.

### Simplified model

```text
Workstate =
    Context
    + Progress
    + Decisions
    + Relationships
    + Open Work
    + Provenance
    + Purpose
```

### Important limitation

A Portable Workstate transfers structured context.

It does not transfer:

- human consciousness;
- lived experience;
- guaranteed understanding;
- authority that was not explicitly granted;
- private material that is not included.

### Related concepts

- AI Continuity
- Freeze State
- Memory Bank
- Context Reconstruction

# S

## Shared Entity

### Definition

A poetic or philosophical description of the continuity and creative process
that may emerge between Psycedelic and AI.

### Boundary

The term should not be interpreted literally.

It does not mean that:

- Psycedelic and AI are one consciousness;
- AI has human experience;
- AI has human rights or human responsibility;
- AI replaces the human participant;
- human authority has been transferred to AI.

### Recommended usage

Use the term when discussing shared creative continuity or working identity.

Avoid using it as a technical claim about consciousness or independent agency.

## Symbiosis

### Definition

A model of human-AI collaboration in which human experience and AI-supported
analysis contribute different but complementary perspectives.

### Human contribution

- meaning;
- purpose;
- judgment;
- values;
- direction;
- lived experience.

### AI contribution

- analysis;
- structure;
- research;
- memory support;
- synthesis;
- comparison;
- documentation.

### Core principle

> Human meaning gives direction. AI helps create structure.

### Boundary

Symbiosis does not mean that AI becomes human or that human responsibility is
removed.

### Status

Conceptual and experimental.

### Related concepts

- PsycedelicAI
- Human Perspective
- AI
- Human Authority
- AI Continuity

## Synthetic Continuity Test

### Definition

A structured test of whether an AI system can reconstruct and continue a
project using supplied context rather than relying only on the immediate
conversation.

### Possible test material

- project identity;
- prior decisions;
- Portable Workstate;
- metadata;
- source documents;
- terminology;
- constraints;
- expected next actions.

### Possible evaluation areas

- context retention;
- decision preservation;
- terminology consistency;
- recognition of uncertainty;
- correct authority boundaries;
- ability to continue the work;
- avoidance of invented context.

### Status

Experimental.

### Limitation

A successful test is evidence about that test condition.

It is not automatically proof of universal continuity across all models,
platforms or tasks.

# T

## Trust Architecture

### Definition

A structured approach to deciding who or what may be trusted, under which
conditions, with which capabilities and within which boundaries.

### Areas of concern

- identity;
- context;
- permissions;
- devices;
- locations;
- zones;
- credentials;
- monitoring;
- approval;
- revocation;
- audit;
- recovery.

### Core principle

Trust should not be treated as a permanent yes-or-no property.

It may depend on:

- identity;
- action;
- context;
- time;
- location;
- device;
- authority;
- current system state.

### Related concepts

- High-Security Facility Concept
- Trust Zone
- Human Authority
- Identity
- Permissions
- Verification

## Trust Zone

### Definition

A defined area of operation with its own access rules, capabilities,
assumptions, monitoring and response requirements.

A trust zone may be:

- physical;
- digital;
- organisational;
- procedural;
- conceptual.

### Purpose

To avoid treating every user, device, action or environment as equally trusted.

### Possible zone properties

- who may enter;
- what may be accessed;
- which actions are permitted;
- what verification is required;
- how activity is monitored;
- what happens when trust is reduced;
- how recovery is performed.

### Related concepts

- Trust Architecture
- Identity
- Permissions
- Recovery
- Verification

# U

## Uncertainty

### Definition

Information that is incomplete, disputed, unverified, ambiguous or dependent
on assumptions.

### Importance

Uncertainty should remain visible rather than being silently converted into
confidence.

### Useful distinctions

```text
Known
    Directly supported

Likely
    Supported by several indicators

Possible
    Plausible but not sufficiently supported

Unknown
    Not currently established
```

### Related concepts

- Fact
- Interpretation
- Provenance
- Validation

---

# V

## Validation

### Definition

The process of checking whether a claim, output, method or implementation
works as described.

### Validation may include

- source comparison;
- testing;
- reproduction;
- human review;
- technical inspection;
- cross-model comparison;
- external feedback;
- documented results.

### Important distinction

A concept can be valuable without being fully validated.

A test result provides evidence under specific conditions.

It does not automatically prove universal capability.

---

## Verification

### Definition

The process of checking identity, authority, state, source, integrity or
conditions before relying on information or allowing an action.

### Verification may ask

- Who provided this?
- What is the source?
- Is the source current?
- Has the content changed?
- Who is authorised to approve this?
- Does the current context match?
- Are required conditions present?
- What should happen if verification fails?

### Related concepts

- Trust Architecture
- Human Approval
- Provenance
- Intent and Authority
- Recovery

---

# W

## Working State

### Definition

The practical state of an ongoing task or project at a given moment.

It may include:

- what is being worked on;
- what has already been completed;
- what decisions have been made;
- what remains open;
- what constraints apply;
- what should happen next.

### Difference from project history

Working state focuses on continuation.

History focuses on development over time.

### Related concepts

- Current State
- Portable Workstate
- Freeze State
- Memory Bank
